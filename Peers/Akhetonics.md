## Snapshot

- **Organisation:** Akhetonics (Germany)
- **Role in this landscape:** high-ambition technology and narrative comparator; not a like-for-like near-term competitor.
- **Publicly stated direction:** all-optical, cross-domain processor architecture combining optical digital, analogue, and quantum functions; optical data interface and optical memory are stated long-term architecture elements.
- **Evidence checked:** 2026-10-10.

## Their public record

Akhetonics describes an all-optical "Reasoning Processing Unit" and an optical interface in which data remains in the optical domain. Its technical material explicitly identifies nonlinear optical components, optical memory, integration, and control as central challenges. These remain company statements and technology ambitions rather than independently verified product specifications.

Sources: [Akhetonics overview](https://www.akhetonics.com/), [technology description](https://www.akhetonics.com/technology), [project updates](https://www.akhetonics.com/blog).

## Independent technical evidence

Akhetonics names Michael Kissner and Leonardo Del Bino as co-founders. Their 2024 *Journal of Lightwave Technology* paper, ["An All-Optical General-Purpose CPU and Optical Computer Architecture"](https://doi.org/10.1109/JLT.2024.3458459), presents an integrated photonic implementation and a proposed URISC-based architecture. Kissner is first author; Del Bino is second author; neither is last author.

Del Bino's earlier experimental work provides a more direct scientific precursor for all-optical state, switching, and memory: ["Optical memories and switching dynamics of counterpropagating light states in microresonators"](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.417951), *Optics Express* (2021). He is first author, while Pascal Del'Haye is senior last author. These sources substantiate the founders' relevant research line, but they do **not** independently validate a complete commercial Akhetonics processor or its advertised performance.

## What this means for our project

For our project, Akhetonics is valuable because its long-term objective overlaps philosophically with our wish to avoid unnecessary domain conversions. Our SBS spin-off has a deliberately more incremental entry: start from an experimentally demonstrated OEO/SBS platform, validate applications with analogue inputs and compact outputs, and only then pursue deeper optical closure or integration.

## Our positioning relative to Akhetonics

We can be ambitious about a new photonic-computing role without promising an all-optical general-purpose computer in 18 months. Akhetonics illustrates both the attractiveness and the high integration burden of that broader vision.

## Our risks and checks

- If full optical-loop closure or optical memory is presented as a PoC deliverable without a credible resource plan, the proposal would overreach toward a far more difficult systems problem. Those directions remain longer-term features.
- If the SBS platform requires high pump power, frequency control, or external electronics that cannot be reduced, its near-term value must be demonstrated through task and systems fit rather than by asserting all-optical operation.
