## Application in brief

Optical inspection and spectroscopy use time-dependent optical intensity or spectral signatures to infer material, process, or environmental state. A reservoir might classify, detect anomalies, or estimate state directly from a suitable optical temporal signal.

## Why it fits the project

- Candidate inputs are optical or can arise close to optical measurement hardware.
- The system's nonlinear dynamics and memory may be useful for evolving spectra or process-dependent optical signatures.
- This direction could overlap with industrial monitoring and laser-process applications.

## Bottlenecks and risks

- If no concrete modality and task are selected, then “spectroscopy” remains too broad to define an input, output, benchmark, or partner.
- If the informative signal does not require temporal memory or nonlinear transformation, then a TDRC adds no value beyond ordinary filtering or static classification.
- If spectral coverage, wavelength, optical power, or temporal bandwidth are incompatible with [[Technical_Specification]], then the present SBS platform cannot access the required signal.
- If the relevant spectral information is available only after a detector and digital spectral reconstruction, then the application does not satisfy the native-optical-input principle in [[Project_Memory#C-009 — Critical assessment of analogue radio-over-fibre]].
- If labelled data, an appropriate baseline, or partner access to an instrument are unavailable, then the claimed use case cannot be validated.
