## Section 1a — The Idea: Breakthrough Innovation Potential

### 1a.i. Brief description of the idea to be taken to proof of concept

#### The problem

The growth of artificial intelligence, sensing and communications is increasing demand for fast processing of continuous data streams. Yet the dominant digital-computing paradigm faces mounting pressure from energy consumption, data movement and dependence on increasingly complex semiconductor hardware. For many time-dependent tasks, large volumes of data are repeatedly converted, moved and processed electronically before a simple decision or control action is produced.

Optical neuromorphic computing offers a different path: use the dynamics of light itself to transform information at high speed and with the prospect of lower energy consumption. Its promise is greatest when computation can occur directly on analogue optical signals, before data are fully digitised and transferred to conventional processors. What is missing is a flexible physical platform that can turn this promise into a route towards real applications.

#### The solution

This Proof of Concept will establish a new SBS-enhanced photonic reservoir-computing platform for direct processing of dynamic optical signals. The platform will use the interaction of light and sound in optical fibre to create a reconfigurable physical processor with memory, nonlinearity and spectral selectivity.

Rather than reproducing a large electronic neural network in optics, the system exploits the natural dynamics of an optoelectronic oscillator to process temporal information. It will be developed to transform complex input streams into compact outputs: predictions, classifications, anomaly flags or control signals. This creates a credible route to offload selected temporal-processing functions from power-hungry digital hardware while retaining electronics where they add most value.

The project will demonstrate a configurable multichannel processor and investigate pump-level control of its computational dynamics. These capabilities are enabling mechanisms, not ends in themselves: they are intended to expand the range of temporal tasks that the same photonic processor can address and to support future adaptation to partner-specific signal environments.

The idea originates from the ERC project **SOUND-PC — Computing with Light and Sound**, led by Birgit Stiller at the Max Planck Society. SOUND-PC has established the scientific basis for exploiting coupled light–sound dynamics in highly nonlinear fibre. The proposed PoC translates this foundation into a photonic computing platform with a clear innovation and application pathway.

### 1a.ii. Demonstration of Breakthrough Innovation Potential

The proposed innovation is a step towards computing with physical dynamics rather than relying exclusively on scaling digital semiconductor systems. It combines the speed and bandwidth of photonics with the temporal memory and nonlinear transformation required for neuromorphic processing.

SBS is the key differentiator. It enables the processor to be configured through an optical pump, providing a route to control filtering, gain and nonlinear dynamics in the same physical system. This makes it possible to explore parallel wavelength channels, task-specific operating regimes and, exploratorily, pump-programmed input masking. Together, these controls can turn a laboratory oscillator into a versatile photonic temporal processor.

The platform is relevant wherever valuable information already exists as a fast analogue optical signal. Potential pathways include analogue radio-over-fibre, industrial laser-process monitoring, distributed fibre sensing and optical ranging. The project will deliberately investigate several of these directions rather than prematurely select one market. It will engage industrial and societal partners, identify real signal-processing bottlenecks, obtain application requirements and, where possible, evaluate representative data. The objective is to find the first application in which photonic neuromorphic processing delivers a decisive system-level advantage.

Existing digital signal processors and conventional optical components remain powerful alternatives. The proposed platform will be valuable if it can perform a temporal task with less electronic data movement, lower energy demand or a more compact system architecture. The PoC will therefore focus on a compelling demonstration of capability and on identifying the application context where that capability matters most.

### 1a.iii. Demonstration of the high-risk/high-gain idea

The high-gain opportunity is to create a foundation for photonic accelerators that process dynamic information directly in the optical domain. If successful, the project will open a route from fundamental light–sound physics to compact processors for sensing, communication and industrial-control systems.

The PoC will validate this opportunity through established temporal tasks, including nonlinear channel equalisation, chaotic time-series prediction and multivariate prediction. These tasks provide rigorous evidence that the platform can exploit memory and nonlinearity, while the multichannel architecture tests whether the same system can process richer information streams or several tasks in parallel.

The scientific risk is genuine: the new SBS-enabled controls must deliver stable, useful computational dynamics rather than only optical functionality. The translational risk is equally important: an attractive laboratory result must map to a real optical signal and a valuable compact output. Addressing both risks is the purpose of the PoC. By combining ambitious photonic computing with structured partner discovery, the project will establish the technical and commercial basis for a future spin-off in optical neuromorphic computing.
