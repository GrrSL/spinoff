## Section 1b.i — Approach and Methodology to Explore the Innovation Potential

### From frontier research to a photonic-computing opportunity

The project will follow a **spin-off-oriented technology-validation pathway**. Its purpose is to transform the light–sound physics developed in SOUND-PC into a credible photonic-computing opportunity: a configurable hardware platform, a validated computational capability, and an evidence-based understanding of where that capability creates value.

The pathway combines three activities that must proceed together. First, the project will validate that SBS adds controllable computational functionality to an established time-delay reservoir architecture. Second, it will demonstrate that this functionality can address demanding temporal-processing tasks. Third, it will engage potential end users and technology partners to identify application settings in which direct optical processing can reduce electronic processing burden or enable a new system function.

This pathway is more appropriate than immediate licensing or a product-specific development programme. The platform has strong scientific foundations, but its first high-value application cannot be selected from laboratory performance alone. It depends on the location of the optical integration point, the nature of the available analogue signal, the value of a compact output, and the constraints of an end user's workflow. The PoC will therefore create the evidence and network needed to make that selection.

### Innovation pathway

The project will progress through the following sequence:

1. **Establish a configurable computational platform.** The SBS-enhanced OEO will be operated as a time-delay reservoir with controllable dynamical regimes and multichannel capability. The aim is to show that the platform is not merely an optical oscillator, but a reconfigurable processor for temporal information.

2. **Demonstrate computational value.** The platform will be evaluated on nonlinear channel equalisation, chaotic time-series prediction and multivariate time-series prediction. These tasks test nonlinear transformation, temporal memory, parallel operation and the use of richer input information. They also provide a common technical language for discussion with prospective partners.

3. **Discover and validate application pathways.** The project will approach relevant industrial and societal organisations, map their signal-processing challenges, and assess whether the platform can operate on a native analogue optical input and produce a compact decision, feature or control output. This will turn broad application hypotheses into specific use cases with measurable requirements.

4. **Prepare the route to exploitation.** The project will assess the intellectual-property position, define the most promising integration route, and develop the business and partnership case for a future spin-off. The intended outcome is an informed commercialisation decision, not a premature claim of a finished product.

### Why spin-off formation is the preferred pathway

A spin-off is the most suitable vehicle because the technology's value will arise from system-level integration across photonics, electronics, data processing and end-user equipment. It requires rapid iteration with potential users, access to application data, and partnerships with organisations that provide optical components, system integration or domain expertise.

Licensing may become appropriate once a defined component, architecture or application has been validated. At the present stage, however, a spin-off pathway provides the flexibility to develop the platform, establish strategic partnerships and pursue the highest-value opportunity identified through the PoC.
