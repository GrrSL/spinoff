# External Optical Input: Seed/Pump Conditioning Limitation

## Why this matters

The laboratory SBS reservoir creates its seed and pump from one laser. This gives the two optical fields a controlled frequency relationship, so they can satisfy the required **9.73 GHz** Brillouin separation. A deployable system may instead receive an external optical signal whose source, wavelength, linewidth, power, and frequency stability are outside the reservoir's control.

The deployment goal is to accept a useful external optical signal without requiring a costly or impractical custom optical source at every installation.

## What an integration module would need to do

- Preserve the external optical signal as the seed, or derive a suitable seed from it.
- Generate a pump with the required frequency separation and adequate power.
- Keep the seed-pump relationship stable enough for SBS interaction.
- Avoid destroying the information that makes the external signal useful.

## Bottlenecks and risks

- If the external source linewidth, wavelength drift, or optical power falls outside the operating tolerance of the SBS configuration, then the SBS interaction can become weak, unstable, or incorrectly positioned in frequency; the reservoir will not reliably process the external signal.
- If seed and pump are generated by independent lasers, then their relative frequency can drift; without active stabilisation, the separation can move away from the 9.73 GHz Brillouin condition and suppress or distort the intended SBS response.
- If the pump is derived from the external input laser, then splitting, modulation, filtering, and control hardware are required; that extra hardware can reduce the expected energy, footprint, and integration benefits.
- If conditioning changes the external waveform's amplitude, phase, frequency content, or timing in an uncontrolled way, then the signal may no longer retain the information required for the computational task.
- If a target application uses a wavelength band, optical power range, or modulation format incompatible with the current fibre and components, then the laboratory SBS platform cannot be connected directly to that application.
- If the conditioning module consumes more energy or space than the electronic processing it replaces, then the system-level value proposition is undermined even if SBS processing works physically.

## Validation needed

- Define the acceptable seed linewidth, wavelength-stability, and power ranges for the current SBS platform.
- Compare a common-laser-derived pump with an independently generated pump and measure the stabilisation burden.
- Identify one target application whose external optical signal can be conditioned with limited additional hardware.
