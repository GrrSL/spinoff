# ERC Proof of Concept Lump Sum Grant 2026 — Part B Preparation Structure

This is a working checklist based on the proposal template. It records what needs to be prepared and does not replace the substantive proposal narrative.

## 0. Document-wide requirements

- Prepare a Part B proposal for **ERC-2026-PoC**.
  - The first page must show the proposal's full title, `ERC Proof of Concept Lump Sum Grant 2026 (ERC-2026-PoC)`, and `Part B`.
- Follow the required formatting.
  - Use Times New Roman, Arial, or a similar font; at least 11 pt.
  - Use 2.0 cm side margins and 1.5 cm top and bottom margins.
  - Use single line spacing.
- Respect the page limits.
  - The main proposal must not exceed 10 pages.
  - Headers, the abstract, references, and the risk-mitigation table are excluded.
  - Section 1a: maximum 3 pages, excluding the abstract and first-page heading material.
  - Section 1b: maximum 6 pages.
  - Section 1c: maximum 1 page, excluding the risk-mitigation table.

## 1. Abstract

- Write a short, precise abstract that explains the objectives and how they will be achieved.
- Make it comprehensible for evaluators and non-specialist programme stakeholders.
- Do not include confidential information: a funded proposal's abstract will be published.
- Meet the formal requirements.
  - Write in English.
  - Use the exact same text as in the administrative form.
  - Stay within 2,000 characters, including spaces.
  - Use plain text; avoid formulae and special characters.

## 2. Section 1a — The idea: Breakthrough Innovation Potential

- Maximum length: 3 pages.
- **1a.i — Brief description of the idea to be taken to proof of concept.**
  - Describe the problem or need the idea addresses.
  - Explain the solution.
    - How will the idea solve or alleviate the problem or need?
    - What difference will it make?
    - State a clear value proposition, including how users' problems are solved or their situation is improved.
  - Describe the origin.
    - Briefly present the ERC-funded project from which the idea is substantially drawn.
    - Demonstrate the relationship between the idea and that ERC-funded project.
- **1a.ii — Demonstration of Breakthrough Innovation Potential.**
  - Explain how the idea can drive innovation and business inventiveness and/or address societal challenges.
  - Describe in detail how project outcomes will be innovative or distinctive.
  - Explain why the proposed solution is new compared with existing solutions.
  - State a clear value proposition.
    - Why should potential users or sponsors choose this solution over existing alternatives?
  - Describe competing products or solutions.
- **1a.iii — Demonstration of the high-risk/high-gain idea.**
  - Explain how success would result in breakthrough innovation.
  - Describe difficult-to-overcome aspects and features that may require new approaches.

## 3. Section 1b — Approach and Methodology

- Maximum length: 6 pages.
- **1b.i — Outline the approach and methodology to explore the innovation potential of ERC-funded research.**
  - Describe the chosen pathway from research to innovation.
    - Examples include patenting, creating a spin-out, licensing, research contracts or collaborations, consultancy, informal advice, public engagement, and policy contributions.
  - Explain why this pathway was chosen.
- **1b.ii — Activities exploring the pathway from ground-breaking research towards innovation.**
  - Describe the planned translation activities.
  - Where applicable, cover:
    - testing, experimentation, demonstration, and validation of the effectiveness of the outcomes or idea;
      - include technical reports or another appropriate form of validation;
      - show that the solution is effective, efficient, sustainable, or otherwise appropriate;
    - research needed to carry out these activities and address weaknesses identified by them;
    - the IPR and knowledge-transfer position and strategy;
      - for example: patenting, spin-out creation, licensing, contracts, collaborations, consultancy, public engagement, or policy contributions;
    - involvement of industrial partners, societal or cultural organisations, policymakers, and other stakeholders.
      - Describe the level and nature of existing relationships.
      - Where relevant contacts already exist, include letters of support or intent as PDF annexes.
    - assessment of potential end users.
      - Who will use the output?
      - What is the expected size of the user group or market?
- **1b.iii — Plan of the proof of concept: Description of the Action.**
  - Account for the funding framework.
    - The action is funded as a standard lump sum pre-fixed at EUR 150,000.
  - Provide a detailed project plan.
    - Define clear objectives and the work to be performed.
    - Demonstrate that the timescale and resources are appropriate and feasible.
    - Describe the resources and the team.
  - Describe the Action and timescale.
    - Demonstrate that the planned activities can be delivered within the project period.
    - Justify the plan through activities, where applicable.
      - validation of results;
      - real-world testing;
      - IPR or knowledge-transfer clarification;
      - competitive and market analysis;
      - engagement with commercial and/or societal partners.
    - For every activity, define objectives, tasks, and completion milestones.
    - Include a Gantt chart and/or milestones table enabling effective monitoring across the project period.
  - Describe resources.
    - Give a narrative description of resources allocated to every activity.
      - project staff and their roles;
      - estimated effort in person-months;
      - equipment and consumables;
      - travel;
      - other resources needed for implementation.
    - Justify that resources are appropriate for the planned work.
    - Do not state costs or any financial figures.
    - Demonstrate good use of the lump sum through the resource description.
  - Describe the team, including the PI.
    - Present the team's relevant achievements and experience.
    - State every member's role in the project.
    - Identify the team's principal strengths and weaknesses.

## 4. Section 1c — Principal Investigator: strategic lead and project management

- Maximum length: 1 page, excluding the risk-mitigation table.
- **Strategic Lead.**
  - Describe how the PI will provide strategic leadership for the project.
- **Project Management.**
  - Describe the organisational structure and decision-making process.
  - Explain why they are appropriate to the project's complexity and scale.
  - Describe what could go wrong and how risks will be identified, accepted, or mitigated.
    - Cover scientific risks, such as a failed test or experiment.
  - Plan for unforeseen non-scientific and non-technical events.
    - Include back-up procedures, emergency response, and ex-post recovery.
    - Examples include the departure of a key team member or loss of access to a facility.
  - Provide the risk-mitigation table.
    - For every risk, state the risk description and proposed mitigation measures.

## 5. Supporting material

- Prepare PDF annexes where relevant stakeholder contacts already exist.
  - Letters of support.
  - Letters of intent.
- Prepare a Gantt chart and/or milestones table for Section 1b.iii.
- Prepare bibliographic references if cited.
